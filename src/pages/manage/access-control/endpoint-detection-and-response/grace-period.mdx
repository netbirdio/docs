# Grace Period for Devices Without a Recent Check-In

By default, NetBird approves a peer in an EDR or MDM integration only while the platform reports its device with a recent check-in.
A device that was switched off for longer than the integration's sync window, such as a laptop left at home during a
two-week vacation, comes back with a check-in that is too old. Its EDR agent usually checks in again within minutes of
starting, but until it does, NetBird keeps the peer off the network.

The grace period covers that gap. When a peer whose device has no recent check-in comes online, NetBird keeps it
connected for a duration you choose, so the agent can check in while the user starts working. Each device gets one
grace period per absence, and gets the next one only after the platform reports it compliant again.

<Note>
    The grace period is available for all EDR and MDM integrations: CrowdStrike Falcon, FleetDM, Huntress, Microsoft Intune, and SentinelOne Singularity. It is off by default.
</Note>

In this guide, a **peer** is the NetBird client on a machine, and a **device** is that machine as your EDR or MDM
platform reports it. NetBird matches the two by the device's serial number, and for FleetDM by its hardware UUID first.

## When a Device Has No Recent Check-In

Every integration has a **sync window**: how recently a device must have checked in with the platform for NetBird to
trust its compliance state. The default and minimum is 24 hours. You set it with the sync window setting of FleetDM,
Huntress, Intune, and SentinelOne. CrowdStrike Falcon uses 24 hours.

NetBird reads the devices that checked in with the platform during the last 30 days and handles each peer in one of
these ways:

| What the platform reports for the device | Result for the peer | Reason shown | Grace period |
|------------------------------------------|---------------------|--------------|--------------|
| Checked in within the sync window and passes your compliance checks | Approved | None | Not needed |
| Checked in within the sync window and fails a compliance check | Denied, and peers added with SSO login are logged out | The failed check | No |
| Last check-in is older than the sync window | Denied | `STALE_CHECK_IN` | Yes |
| No check-in during the last 30 days, never enrolled, or removed from the platform | Denied | `NOT_FOUND_IN_EDR` | Yes |
| More than one device has the peer's serial number or hardware UUID | Denied | `DUPLICATE_MATCH` | Never |

The grace period only covers a missing check-in. A device that keeps checking in and fails your compliance checks
doesn't get one. If such a device stops checking in for longer than the sync window, NetBird can no longer tell its
current state, so it gets a grace period like any other device without a recent check-in. If its next check-in still
fails your compliance checks, NetBird denies the peer again.

## What Happens Without a Grace Period

When a device's check-in is too old, or the platform no longer reports the device:

- The peer loses network access as soon as NetBird notices, at the next sync or when the peer connects. The peers list
  marks it as `Approval required`, with the reason in the tooltip.
- The peer stays logged in. A missing check-in is not a compliance failure, and logging in again cannot fix it, so
  NetBird does not expire the peer's login the way it does for a non-compliant device.
- Once the device checks in again and passes your compliance checks, NetBird approves the peer at the next sync. The
  user doesn't need to reconnect or log in again.

## How the Grace Period Works

### When It Starts

The grace period starts the first time NetBird sees the peer online while its device has no recent check-in:

- when the peer connects, or
- at a sync, when the peer is already connected and its device's last check-in has just become older than the sync
  window.

An offline peer does not use its grace period. While a laptop sits switched off, its peer is denied with
`STALE_CHECK_IN`, and the grace period starts only when the laptop connects again.

### How Long It Lasts

The grace period lasts the configured duration, from 1 to 240 minutes, counted from the moment it starts.
Disconnecting and reconnecting doesn't restart or extend it. If you change the duration, the change also applies to
grace periods that are already running.

### When It Ends

- **The device checks in and passes your compliance checks.** NetBird approves the peer as usual at the next sync. The
  grace period is over, and the device can get a new one on its next absence.
- **The device checks in and fails a compliance check.** NetBird denies the peer at the next sync, as it does for any
  non-compliant device.
- **The duration runs out.** The peer loses network access at the next sync but stays logged in. It gets access back
  once the device checks in and passes your compliance checks.
- **You turn the grace period off.** Grace periods that are running end at the next sync.

### When a Device Gets Another One

A device gets one grace period per absence. After its grace period runs out, the peer stays denied until the device
checks in and passes your compliance checks. Reconnecting, waiting, or adding another peer on the same device doesn't
give it a second one. The next time the device stays away longer than the sync window, it gets a new grace period.

## Example

The sync window is 24 hours and the grace period is 30 minutes. Jane's laptop was switched off for a week.

| Time | What happens | Jane's network access |
|------|--------------|-----------------------|
| During the week | The laptop's last check-in becomes older than 24 hours. Its peer is offline, so NetBird denies it with `STALE_CHECK_IN` and keeps the grace period for later. | None, the laptop is off |
| Monday, 09:00 | Jane switches the laptop on and NetBird connects. The grace period starts and runs until 09:30. | Yes |
| Monday, 09:04 | The EDR agent checks in and the laptop passes the compliance checks. At the next sync, NetBird approves the peer as usual. | Yes |

If the agent had not checked in by 09:30, for example because it was uninstalled, the grace period would end at 09:30
and Jane would lose network access at the next sync after that. She would get it back once the agent checks in and the
laptop passes the compliance checks. Reconnecting would not give her more time.

## Grace Period for New Peers

A **new peer** is a peer that NetBird has never approved or denied through the integration, usually one that was just
added. The **Include new peers** option decides whether new peers get a grace period:

- **Off (default).** A new peer whose device is not in the platform yet, or hasn't checked in recently, is denied
  without a grace period. It stays without one, even after NetBird has denied it, until the platform reports its device
  compliant. From then on, the peer gets a grace period on later absences like any other peer.
- **On.** A new peer gets one grace period too, under the same rules as any other peer.

Turn the option on only if your devices routinely join NetBird before your EDR or MDM platform knows about them, for
example when the EDR agent is installed after NetBird during onboarding. With the option on, a device that the platform
has never reported can reach your network for the length of the grace period.

<Note>
    When you connect a new integration, every peer in its groups is new until NetBird checks it for the first time. With **Include new peers** off, peers whose devices are missing from the platform or haven't checked in recently stay denied until the platform reports them compliant.
</Note>

## One Grace Period per Device

The grace period belongs to the device, not to the peer. Once a device has used its grace period, and until the
platform reports it compliant again:

- Deleting the peer and adding it again doesn't give the device a new grace period.
- Another peer on the same device, for example a second NetBird profile, doesn't get a grace period of its own.
- A new peer on the device doesn't get one either, even with **Include new peers** on.

Peers that report neither a serial number nor a hardware UUID never get a grace period, because NetBird cannot tie
them to a device. iOS peers are one example.

## Configure the Grace Period

When you connect a new integration, the grace period settings are on the last step of the setup wizard, below the sync
settings.

<p>
    <img src="/docs-static/img/manage/access-control/endpoint-detection-and-response/grace-period/grace-period-setup.png" alt="Grace period settings on the last step of the FleetDM setup wizard" className="imagewrapper-big"/>
</p>

To change them for a connected integration:

1. Go to the [Integrations &raquo; EDR](https://app.netbird.io/integrations?tab=edr) tab.
2. Click **Settings** on the integration.
3. Open the **Settings** tab.
4. Turn on **Grace Period** and set the **Duration** in minutes, from 1 to 240. The default is 30 minutes.
5. Optional. Turn on **Include new peers**. See [Grace Period for New Peers](#grace-period-for-new-peers) before you do.
6. Click **Save Changes**.

<p>
    <img src="/docs-static/img/manage/access-control/endpoint-detection-and-response/grace-period/grace-period-settings.png" alt="Grace period with Include new peers turned on in the FleetDM integration settings" className="imagewrapper-big"/>
</p>

To turn the grace period off, turn off **Grace Period** and save.

## Activity Events

NetBird records these events under **[Audit Events](/manage/activity)**:

| Event | When it is recorded |
|-------|---------------------|
| `Peer kept connected during the EDR grace period` | A grace period started. The event shows the peer and the duration. |
| `Peer denied by integrated validator` | NetBird denied a peer. The event shows the reason, such as no recent EDR check-in, not found in EDR, or the failed compliance check. |
| `Peer approved by integrated validator` | NetBird approved a peer, for example after its device checked in again. |

## Why a Peer Didn't Get a Grace Period

| Cause | What to do |
|-------|------------|
| The grace period is off for the integration. | Turn on **Grace Period** in the integration settings. |
| The peer is offline. | Nothing. The grace period starts when the peer connects. |
| The device already used its grace period during this absence, through this peer or another peer on the same device. | Make sure the EDR agent runs and checks in. The peer gets access back once the device passes your compliance checks. |
| The peer is new and **Include new peers** is off. | Make sure the device is enrolled in your platform, checks in, and passes your compliance checks. Or turn on **Include new peers**. |
| The peer reports no serial number or hardware UUID. | None. Peers that cannot be tied to a device never get a grace period. |
| The device checked in recently and fails a compliance check. | Fix the compliance issue. The grace period only covers a missing check-in. |
| The peer matches more than one device in the platform. | Make sure each device has a unique serial number or hardware UUID. |

To give a peer access right away regardless of its compliance state, [bypass compliance](/manage/access-control/endpoint-detection-and-response/bypass-compliance)
for it.

## Best Practices

- **Keep the duration short.** Set it to about how long your platform's agent takes to check in after a device starts.
  During the grace period, the device has network access without a current report from the platform.
- **Keep Include new peers off** unless your onboarding needs it, so that a device the platform has never reported
  cannot reach your network.
- **Know that removed devices can get a grace period too.** If you remove a device from your platform while its peer
  is online and the device hasn't used its grace period yet, the peer keeps access until its grace period ends. To cut a
  device off right away, delete its peer in NetBird.
- **Make sure devices report unique serial numbers**, so that each peer matches exactly one device in the platform.
- **Review the activity log** for grace period events, to spot devices whose agent regularly fails to check in.
